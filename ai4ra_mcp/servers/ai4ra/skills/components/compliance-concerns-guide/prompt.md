# Compliance Concerns — Guide

> **Purpose:** The fixed list of concerns a statement about a sponsored project can raise, each a name and a description, so that statements about the same thing can be grouped under the same name wherever they appear. Facts and reference only; the order of work is the placement skill's. Names no client, no regulation and no institution.
> **Expected input:** Statements about how a sponsored project is run, paid for or governed: a document, a section, a sentence.
> **Expected output:** Reference for a placement skill: the twenty names and what each covers.

---

## Prompt

Twenty concerns that a statement about a sponsored project can raise. Each is a name and a description of what it covers and where it stops. The names group statements; they say nothing about which rule governs one.

### The list

Costs

1. **Allowability of costs.** Whether a cost may be charged to the award at all: that it is needed for the work and reasonable in amount, that the project is the one that benefits from it, that it is treated the same way as like costs elsewhere, and that it is not a kind of cost the sponsor excludes. The general concern for a statement about charging a cost; a statement that fits one of the narrower cost concerns below belongs to that one.
2. **Indirect costs.** The rate charged for facilities and administration (overhead): which rate applies, the base it is applied to and what that base leaves out, a reduced or waived rate, and a sponsor's limit on it.
3. **Compensation and effort.** Paying people from the award: salaries and wages, fringe benefits, summer and supplemental pay, a limit on salary, the share of a person's time promised to the project, and how the time actually given is recorded and confirmed.
4. **Cost sharing.** What the institution or a third party contributes toward the project's cost beside the sponsor's funds: whether it is required or offered, what may count, how a contribution is valued, and how it is documented.
5. **Cost transfers.** Moving a charge that is already on the books from one project or account to another: when it may be done, how soon after the original charge, and the explanation it needs.
6. **Procurement.** Buying goods and services with award funds: competition, quotes and bids, buying from a single source, the dollar levels at which the method changes, consultants and contractors, and a personal interest in a purchase. An organization that carries out part of the work itself is Subawards.
7. **Subawards.** Passing part of the work and its funds to another organization: whether that organization is a partner in the work or a seller to it, what the agreement must carry, oversight of the partner, and agreements for a fixed amount.
8. **Prior approvals and budget changes.** A change the sponsor must agree to before it happens: moving funds between budget categories, a change of scope, a change of lead investigator or a drop in a key person's time, carrying unspent funds into the next period, and a later end date without new funds.
9. **Period of performance.** The dates the award runs and where a cost falls against them: costs before the start, spending before the award has arrived, and charges after the end.
10. **Program income.** Money the project earns because of the award, such as fees, sales, registration charges and royalties, and how it may be used and must be reported.
11. **Equipment and property.** Lasting items bought or held under the award: what counts as equipment, who holds title, how an item may be used and shared, the inventory kept, and what happens to it when the award ends.
12. **Participant support.** Payments to or for people who take part in a training or an event the project runs and who are not its employees: stipends, travel, subsistence and registration, and keeping those funds apart and for that use.
13. **Travel.** Trips charged to the award: who travels and for what purpose, transport and lodging, daily allowances, a required choice of carrier, and travel abroad. Travel paid for a participant is Participant support.
14. **Payment and cash management.** How funds move from the sponsor to the institution: reimbursement and advance, invoices and their timing, interest earned on an advance, and awards paid as a fixed price or by milestone.
15. **Reporting, records and closeout.** What is owed to the sponsor during and after the award, and what is kept: progress and financial reports with their due dates, how long records are held and who may see them, and the final reports and settling of accounts at the end.

Conduct of the research

16. **Conflict of interest.** A personal financial interest that could bear on the design, conduct or reporting of the work: what must be disclosed and when, how it is reviewed and managed, and what the sponsor is told. A personal interest in one purchase is Procurement.
17. **Export controls and research security.** Controlled items, technology and data, and who may receive them at home or abroad; restricted parties and destinations; foreign appointments, support and talent programs and their disclosure; equipment and services a sponsor prohibits.
18. **Research protocols.** Approvals the work itself needs before it begins and keeps while it runs: research with human participants, with animals, with hazardous biological agents, and research whose results could be misused.
19. **Research data and intellectual property.** Who owns the project's data and what it produces, and who may use them: managing and sharing data, inventions and their reporting, patents, copyright, licensing, and the rights the sponsor keeps. How long financial and administrative records are held is Reporting, records and closeout.
20. **Research integrity.** The honesty of the research record: fabrication, falsification and plagiarism, how an allegation is handled, training in the responsible conduct of research, and authorship.

### What holds for the list

- The list is closed. A name is written exactly as above, so that two readers of the same statement write the same name.
- A name carries no citation. Which regulation, policy or award term governs a statement is found from the statement by whoever holds that source.
- Where two descriptions meet, each says which of them takes the statement. Among the cost concerns the narrower takes it, and Allowability of costs takes what no narrower one covers.
- There is no catch-all. A statement that raises none of the twenty has no name.
- Not in the list, on purpose: audit, and anything that belongs to one sponsor alone, which that sponsor's own terms carry.

---

## Quality Standards

1. **Twenty names, closed**, each written exactly.
2. **Descriptions only**: no regulation, no policy, no institution, no example citation.
3. **Every meeting of two descriptions says which takes the statement.**
